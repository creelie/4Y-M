## 1. The data (Hexagon v7, §§ `ssec:bprefine`, `ssec:bpsplitstrong`, `ssec:bpfibre`)

- **Lattices.** Fine torus Λ_{bL} with links E', coarse torus Λ_L with links E, d = 4, block side b ≥ 3.
- **Block map c.** The straight-path map. For e = (y,ν), the path π(e) is the b links (by + i e_ν, ν), i = 0..b−1, and
  c(U)_e = U_{π(e)_0} ⋯ U_{π(e)_{b−1}}.
- **Off-path links.** E'_0 = E' \ ∪_e π(e), the links on no path. Σ = the sites on paths (block corners and inner points).
- **Section (gauge-fixed form).** Θ(v, m) puts v_e on the mark l(e) ∈ π(e), puts 0 (resp. I) on the other path links, and puts m on E'_0.
  - In the nonabelian setting, Lemma `lem:bprefine`(iii) gives g_U U = Θ(c(U), M(U)), with M independent of c under a Haar refinement.
  - In the abelian Gaussian model, v ∈ R^E, m ∈ N^⊥ ⊂ R^{E'_0}. Here N = {dφ|_{E'_0} : φ = 0 on Σ} is the residual gauge kernel (Lemma `lem:bpfibker`).
- **Curvature operator.** K = dᵀd on R^{E'} (lattice Maxwell), and S(A) = (β/2)|dA|².
  - Blocks: the marks block is 6I (distinct marks are never partners, Prop `prop:bpfibreduce`(i)), K_0 = the E'_0 block, Ψ v = Σ_e v_e (Kδ_{l(e)})|_{E'_0}.
  - Gauge-fixed operator: 𝒜 = K_0 + BBᵀ, with B φ = dφ|_{E'_0}.
- **Effective coarse form.** Γ = 6I − Ψᵀ K_0^+ Ψ = 6I − Ψᵀ𝒜^{−1}Ψ.
  - Bounds 1/b² ≤ Γ_ee ≤ 6/b (Prop `prop:bpfibgauss`).
  - Bloch symbol `eq:blockdecay-symbol`, with decay |Γ^∞_b(w)| ≤ (C/b) e^{−c|w|_1/√b}, sharp in the rate (Thm `thm:blockdecay`).
  - Row sums of order (log b)/b (Thm `thm:bprowgauss`).
- **Fibres.** F_v = c^{−1}(v). In the Gaussian model the fibre law ς_v is the Gaussian on N^⊥ with mean m_v = −𝒜^{−1}Ψv and covariance β^{−1}K_0^+, the same for every v.
- **The three hypotheses of Thm 114** (`thm:bpsplitcond`), with n' ≥ bn, L ≥ 2n+4, bL ≥ 2n'+4:
  - Cubes V'_{x'} have side n'. 𝓕_{x'} = σ(links outside V'_{x'}), 𝓗_{x'} = 𝓕_{x'} ∨ σ(c), and T(x') = {e : π(e) ∩ V'_{x'} ≠ ∅}.
  - Φ(f) = n'^{−4} Σ_{x'} μ'(Var(f | 𝓗_{x'})) is the fibre cube dynamics.
  - (F) μ'(Var(f|c)) ≤ C_F Φ(f).
  - (X) ‖Π_{𝓕_{x'}}(g∘c)‖ ≤ ρ‖g‖ for g ∈ 𝒜^c with P_{T(x')} g = 0.
  - (D) ‖Σ_{x'} w_{x'}‖² ≤ R Σ_{x'} ‖w_{x'}‖², where w_{x'} = (I − Q) Π_{𝓕_{x'}}(g_{x'}∘c).

**Gauge caveat.** In the Gaussian model, "the links outside V'_{x'}" must mean the gauge-invariant linear functionals of A_{E' \ V'_{x'}}. Equivalently, work in the 𝒜-gauge and generate 𝓕_{x'} by the closed subspace of the Gaussian space spanned by those functionals. Everything below is in that convention. The nonabelian statements are not touched.

## 2. (F) and (X) reduce to the first chaos with the same constants

Let H be the Gaussian Hilbert space (the centred linear functionals of the fine field). Every σ-algebra above is generated by a closed subspace of H:
- 𝓕_{x'} by H_{𝓕,x'};
- σ(c) by H_c, the span of the path sums;
- 𝓗_{x'} by H_{𝓕,x'} + H_c.

Write P_• for the orthogonal projection in H. Two standard facts (Janson, *Gaussian Hilbert Spaces*, Thms 2.6 and 4.9):
- L²(μ') = ⊕_{k≥0} H^{:k:}, with H^{:k:} ≅ Sym^k H isometrically up to k!.
- For a σ-algebra generated by a closed subspace with projection P, the conditional expectation is the second quantization Γ(P), which acts as P^{⊗k} on H^{:k:}.

Both sides of (F), (X) and (D) are sums of squared norms of chaos-preserving operators. So each splits into an orthogonal sum over k, and it is enough to prove each inequality chaos by chaos.

**Lemma A1 (F on all of L²).** Let λ_F be the best constant in (F) for f ∈ H (first chaos). Then (F) holds on all of L²(μ') with C_F = λ_F.

*Proof.*
1. Condition on c. The fibre law is a Gaussian with Hilbert space H_0 = H ⊖ H_c, and Var(f|c), Var(f|𝓗_{x'}) are the corresponding fibre quantities. So it suffices to prove, on H_0^{:k:} with the identity as the variance operator,

       I ≤ λ_F · n'^{−4} Σ_{x'} (I − P_{x'}^{⊗k}),

   where P_{x'} is the projection of H_0 onto H_0 ∩ (H_{𝓕,x'} + H_c).
2. P_{x'}^{⊗k} ≤ P_{x'} ⊗ I^{⊗(k−1)}, because P_{x'} ≤ I and the factors commute. So I − P_{x'}^{⊗k} ≥ (I − P_{x'}) ⊗ I^{⊗(k−1)}.
3. Summing over x', n'^{−4} Σ_{x'} (I − P_{x'}^{⊗k}) ≥ 𝒟 ⊗ I^{⊗(k−1)}, with 𝒟 = n'^{−4} Σ_{x'} (I − P_{x'}).
4. The first-chaos hypothesis says exactly 𝒟 ≥ λ_F^{−1} I on H_0. Hence 𝒟 ⊗ I ≥ λ_F^{−1} I. ∎

So the item "extend the variance estimate from linear observables to all of Gaussian L²" in direction A is **closed** for (F): C_F = λ_F(first chaos), with no loss. This is the Gaussian analogue of the classical fact that the Gibbs-sampler gap of a Gaussian is attained on linear functions.

**Lemma A2 (X on all of L²).** Let ρ_1 = sup ‖P_{𝓕,x'} u‖ / ‖u‖ over u ∈ H_c with u ⊥ H_{c,out}, where H_{c,out} is the span of the coarse links outside T(x'). Then (X) holds on all of L²(μ^c) with ρ = ρ_1.

*Proof.*
1. Decompose H_c = H_{c,out} ⊕ C', where C' = H_c ⊖ H_{c,out}.
2. P_{𝓕} fixes H_{c,out}, which is 𝓕-measurable. It maps C' into H_{c,out}^⊥: for u ∈ C' and w ∈ H_{c,out}, ⟨P_𝓕 u, w⟩ = ⟨u, w⟩ = 0.
3. In H_c^{:k:}, the condition P_T g = 0 means g ⊥ Sym^k H_{c,out}. So g = Σ_{j≥1} g_j, where g_j has exactly j factors in C'.
4. P_𝓕^{⊗k} preserves this grading (by step 2) and contracts the j-th piece by at most ρ_1^j ≤ ρ_1.
5. Different j stay orthogonal after applying P_𝓕^{⊗k}, again by step 2 (the j factors land in H_{c,out}^⊥ and the others stay in H_{c,out}). So ‖Γ(P_𝓕) g‖ ≤ ρ_1 ‖g‖. ∎

## 3. (D) does not reduce to the first chaos

On H^{:k:}, w_{x'} = (I − P_c^{⊗k}) P_{𝓕,x'}^{⊗k} g_{x'}, and (D) asks for almost-orthogonality of these vectors across x' with a constant independent of the torus.
- At k = 1 this is a statement about the operators (I − P_c) P_{𝓕,x'} on C'_{x'}. It should follow from the exponential decay of the fibre covariance β^{−1}K_0^+, at rate ~ 1/√b by Thm `thm:blockdecay`. That would give a Schur bound R_1 ≲ Σ_{x''} sup ‖w_{x'}^* w_{x''}‖ / ‖w‖² = O((n' + √b)^4 / n'^4).
- At k ≥ 2, w_{x'} contains mixed tensors, with one factor in the fibre directions near V'_{x'} and the other factors coarse variables anywhere in T(x'). Two such vectors for distant x', x'' overlap through their coarse factors, which are **not** localized. The Schur constant at level k is not obviously bounded by a power of R_1.
- **Open item A-D:** bound R_k uniformly in k, or show that (D) only needs the k = 1 constant after a further projection.
  - One route: replace g_{x'} by its Hoeffding/Efron–Stein component relative to T(x'). Only the component of g_{x'} that is *linear* in C'_{x'} survives (I − Q) P_𝓕.
  - That would reduce (D) to k = 1 with coefficients in L²(coarse), i.e. R = R_1 ⊗ I, if the identity (I − P_c^{⊗k}) P_𝓕^{⊗k} = Σ_j ((I − P_c) P_𝓕)_{(j)} ⊗ (P_c P_𝓕)_{(rest)} can be organized as an orthogonal sum. It cannot naively, because the terms overlap. This is the first thing to do in week 2.

## 4. Computation plan (cluster, ≤ 64 CPUs)

All three first-chaos constants are finite-dimensional linear algebra on a b-periodic structure with a cube cutting it:
- λ_F: the smallest eigenvalue of 𝒟 on H_0.
- ρ_1: a norm of a projection.
- R_1: a Schur sum.

Since n' ≥ 2b and bL ≥ 2n' + 4, the smallest admissible case is b = 3, n = 2, n' = 6, L = 8: fine side 24, 1.3 × 10⁶ links. That is too big for dense algebra.

Plan:
1. **Cube-local reductions.** ρ_1 and each P_{x'} only need the Gaussian conditioned outside a cube. Use the Bloch symbol for the infinite-volume fibre covariance (the 𝒜^{−1} kernel at quasi-momentum κ), and Schur complements on the (n'+2)^4 cube. That is ~ 10⁴ variables, which is dense-feasible.
2. **λ_F.** Use Lanczos on 𝒟, with each P_{x'} applied by a cube solve (CG), exploiting b-periodicity (b⁴ inequivalent cube positions).
3. **Output.** C_F, ρ_1, R_1 for b = 3, 4, 6 and n' = 2b, 3b, then η = ρ(R C_F)^{1/2}/n'² and the B-quantity δ = ρ² + η. The target in B is Σ_j ρ_j² < ∞ along n_j = 2n_{j−1}, so ρ_1 as a function of n'/b is the number that matters most.
